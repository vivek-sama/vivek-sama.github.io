Vanguard ETF Challenge

Protecting a ranking, not chasing returns

The challenge

Each team managed $250,000 of simulated capital in Vanguard ETFs, with at most $62,500 in any one fund, limits on cash, and a minimum number of trades. Teams were ranked on Sharpe ratio, so the goal was the best return for each unit of risk, not the highest return.

The decision that mattered

We began with a mix of growth and dividend equity ETFs (VGT, VYM, VIG). Halfway through, we broke into the top 10. At that point, I decided our job was to protect a strong risk-adjusted position, not to take more risk.

I moved half the portfolio entirely into short-term bond and Treasury ETFs (VBIL, VUSB, VGUS, VGSH). The other half I split evenly between growth and defensive holdings, so about three-quarters of the portfolio was defensive.

When the market fell sharply, many teams lost their gains. Our portfolio held up, and we climbed from the top 10 into the top 5.

As finalists, we're designing an ETF to present to Vanguard on November 13, and we visit the New York Stock Exchange on December 3.

What I learned

The metric you're judged on should shape every decision. Under a Sharpe ranking, cutting volatility was worth more than adding return, and the hardest call was choosing not to chase the rally.
