About

How I got into finance

I ran into finance's central question before I ever took a finance class. In 2024 I started a digital media brand and grew it to more than 50,000 followers in eight months. When I sold it, the price was about $800.

That gap stayed with me. Fifty thousand people were paying attention, yet the business was worth very little, because attention isn't cash flow. I wanted to understand what actually makes a business valuable, and that question led me to finance.

How I work

At the University of Washington, I've learned to test ideas instead of trusting them. My event study asked whether defense stocks beat the market during conflicts. The story sounded convincing; the data said no (p = 0.516). For Nebius, I rebuilt a company's revenue from its data centers, power and GPUs instead of from management guidance. With Vult, I co-founded a platform built to reject trading strategies that only look good before costs.

Where I'm headed

I want to bring that approach to technology investment banking, working on M&A and capital raising, and later to growth equity. The question there is the same one I started with: what is this business really worth?

Outside finance

Outside finance, you'll usually find me in the gym or on a basketball court. I first picked up a basketball when I was five and never really put it down. It's still my favorite thing to play and to follow, and I've been a LeBron fan for as long as I've been watching the game.

Staying active is a fixed part of my week. I plan my schedule around classes and training, not the other way round.
